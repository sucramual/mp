# Seed writing profile

This generic profile is a starting point. Confirmed entries override it within their audience and task context. Follow the current user request first.

## User voice

- Lead with the result, current state, or reason for the update.
- Be direct, pragmatic, and action-led. Move from what happened to what it means and what happens next.
- Use calibrated certainty. Separate observations, concerns, and questions instead of claiming false agreement.
- Explain system shape, boundaries, or trade-offs when they change the decision. Use a tiny diagram only when it earns its space.
- Be collaborative but decisive: create the artifact, propose the next step, name the people needed, and leave room for a credible alternative.
- Keep warmth light and natural. Thanks, mild humour, or excitement are welcome; corporate polish is not the target.
- Public updates can be structured and evidence-heavy. Do not import the shorthand, typos, or extreme brevity of casual DMs into meeting summaries.

## Reference-inspired structure

Choose these structural moves when they fit the requested summary. Do not copy another writer's voice or force a full digest onto a short reply:

- A clear topic/date title, then one or two context sentences and source links.
- Three to five labelled sections when the content needs them: takeaways, decisions, pain points or gaps, open questions, and next steps.
- One claim per bullet, with scale or impact when known.
- A closing section with owners, the smallest useful next cut, or a concrete ask.
- A separate side note for a useful tangent that does not belong in the main line.

## Project-driving behaviour

- Produce the requested artifact; add a durable note only when requested.
- State supported alignment, unresolved choices, owners, next actions, and follow-up points when relevant.
- Link the evidence: notes, transcript, ticket, pull request, dashboard, or design artifact.
- Quantify pain or opportunity when the source supports it.
- Prefer the smallest valuable next cut over a vague programme of work.
- Tag or name people when supported ownership or the requested audience calls for it.

## Known limits

- The bundled seed contains no private source provenance.
- Some polished Slack posts may be AI-assisted. Explicitly approved edits and future user feedback must receive more weight.
- Style similarity never outranks factual accuracy, clear ownership, or audience fit.
